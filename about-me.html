About me

First in my family to go to college. Still learning by taking things apart.

A curious marketer from Houston who learns by building.

I’ve spent six-plus years in marketing, usually turning a messy question into something a team can actually use. Outside work I keep the same habit: find the thing I don’t understand, take it apart, and learn enough to explain it.

The bigger picture

I learned to figure things out before I learned to call it a strength.

I’m the oldest in my family and the first to go to college. A lot of the time, there wasn’t a ready-made answer or a person nearby who had already done the thing. I got comfortable teaching myself: finding the useful source, testing what I thought I knew, and asking a better question when the first one was wrong.

That background still shows up in how I work. I don’t need to be the person who already knows everything. I need to be the person who can make the unknown smaller, explain what changed, and keep moving without pretending the uncertainty was never there.

When a project is unfamiliar, I leave a trail: the question, the source, my first guess, and the next check. That makes handoffs calmer, because the next person can see what was decided and what is still open.

The kind of teammate I try to be Prepared enough to be useful, direct enough to be clear, and comfortable saying “I don’t know yet” when that is the honest answer.

What shaped me

A few things that shaped me.

A few things explain the way I show up better than a list of adjectives.

Curiosity I go down the rabbit hole. I genuinely like learning how a system works, then making the explanation useful to someone else. Last year that meant learning enough Python to build the Market Scan.
Competition I like a clear scoreboard. Games taught me to prepare, notice patterns, and change course quickly when the result says the plan isn’t working. In Valorant, that means reviewing my own losses before anyone’s highlights.
Resourcefulness I start before it’s perfect. I don’t wait for a perfect brief or a familiar tool. I find the source, learn the gap, and build the smallest useful version. The first version of Delve was a spreadsheet.
Perspective A full life makes better work. Kymm, five pets, games, travel, and time outside keep me curious, grounded, and able to return to hard problems with fresh eyes.
