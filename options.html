Options research · simulation only

I built a research lab that tests stock-market ideas without risking a dollar.

It studies options, contracts that let someone buy or sell a stock at a set price later. Each idea is written down, replayed against past market data, and challenged by fair comparisons. Most ideas fail, and that is the point.

It has run 15,412 historical tests, about 270 days of nonstop computer time. By my rough estimate it needs about 15 minutes of my attention a day.

The result 446 ideas tested; 11 passed every check. Not one dollar of real money has ever been placed. The standing decision is to keep everything on paper.

The problem

Finding something interesting is easy. Knowing whether it's real is the job.

A test on past market data can draw a beautiful line that means nothing, especially when nobody wrote the question down first. A convincing chart isn't proof.

This project makes challenging the result part of the routine: a limited list of questions, fair comparisons, simulated money only, and a record of everything that got rejected.

In plain terms

An idea has to survive the whole loop.

01Write the question. Say exactly what the idea should explain.
02Decide what would prove it wrong. Set the comparison and failure rule first.
03Replay history. Test against past market data and keep the records.
04Challenge the result. Compare it with a simpler explanation, simulated money only.
05Record what happened. Pass, fail, no real pattern, or too little data, all dated.
06Check before the next run. See what changed, and keep real money out.

The scale of the check

Each conclusion rests on a much bigger pile of tests.

Every conclusion sits on top of thousands of historical tests, run over 6,479.2 hours of computer time. That is about 270 days of a computer working nonstop.

System checks

The research is only as trustworthy as the system running it.

The steps that produce a conclusion get checked on a schedule, not only when a result looks surprising.

Tests 952 automated tests pass; they exercise 48.8% of the code. That's a safety net, not full coverage.
Problems caught 11 problems logged; the typical one was noticed within 24.0 hours. Each stays in the log with what broke and when it was caught.
One example A broken run was kept out of the results. A health check stopped an incomplete run before any conclusion was recorded.
Automation 34 scheduled tasks keep it running. I read results instead of watching every step; what the system may do stays the same.
What it took

A passing result is still not proof.

How long
Built mostly between late July and mid-September 2026, a rough estimate from the project's file dates. It keeps running on its own schedule.
What broke
An incomplete run. It was repeated after the fix, and the problem stayed in the log.
Do differently
Show the untested gaps sooner, and make "not enough evidence" as easy to see as a pass.
Evidence record

Every number on this page, with its source.

Open the full evidence record

This public record contains counts and process measures only. Strategy names, which stocks were tested, cutoffs, position sizes, and any result that could reveal a winning idea are deliberately left out. The checked summary stays public; the larger working record remains private.

One row is one recorded verdict, not one trade. A single idea can be tested many times, and every run is written down whether it passed or failed. Nothing here was sized for real money.
