Market Scan · personal project

What 683 demand gen job posts actually ask for.

Job posts are the most direct description of what companies want from a demand gen marketer, and about 120 new ones appear each week. I built a tool that gathers them from 33 sources and scores each one against a short written checklist.

The first version quietly threw away most of the good posts. Finding out why taught me the same lesson as lead scoring: a short list only means something if you check what disappeared.

See how it works ↓ See the evidence ↓
The result 71% of demand gen posts ask for attribution or measurement skills. Counted across the 615 posts the tool could score, from 683 collected over about six weeks in summer 2026. 57 matched every item on the checklist.

Why I built it

Scoring is cheap. Reading is the expensive part.

Reading every post is slow, and skimming misses the pattern. A written checklist can do the first pass on every post, so the reading goes to what the whole pile says.

What broke first

The first run found six ways to lose a good posting.

The first run searched 117 job boards and kept only four jobs. There were plenty of postings; six separate filters were quietly throwing good ones away. It also showed that 84 of those boards either blocked automated collection or only reposted listings from bigger boards, so the tool now reads 33 sources directly.

I traced and fixed each one before trusting the list.

What the checklist is

Four plain items. One point each.

The checklist is short: the kind of work, the level of ownership, the marketing channels involved, and practical requirements like location. A post’s score is how many of those show up in its title or description. A post that clearly matches several items beats one that mentions a single keyword in passing.

Why that is enough It’s deliberately simple: a fast first pass through 683 posts, so what’s left to read is the patterns.
What the market asks for

What the posts say about the job.

Counted across all 615 scored posts. The surprise: only 1 in 9 mentioned lead quality or the Sales handoff, even though most asked for pipeline results.

71% mention attribution, measurement, or reporting
Salesforce the most-requested platform, named in 64% of posts
38% include owning a budget
46% open to remote work
The lead-scoring lesson A checklist that ranks posts is a lead-scoring model with a different input. The six filters that dropped good posts are the same leaks that drop good leads: a field mapped wrong, a location rule set too tight, duplicate removal that eats real records.
What it took

A useful list needed more than a score.

How long
Built between early July and mid-August 2026, going by the project’s file dates.
Do differently
Log every post a filter throws away from day one, so a short list never looks good before the missing ones are explained.
