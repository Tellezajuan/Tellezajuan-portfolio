Marketing Measurement · public ad data

Most campaigns that looked worth scaling only looked good in hindsight.

Ad reports keep crediting results to a campaign for weeks after its budget was decided. I rebuilt 675 campaign reports to show only what was known on the day each decision was due.

On decision day, only 39% of the eventual credit existed. I wrote the plan first, then tried hard to break the result.

Read the finding ↓ See the evidence ↓
The result 167 of 285 campaigns that looked worth scaling only looked good because of credit that arrived after the budget was set. At the extreme, the 25 campaigns with the most late credit looked about 4× cheaper in hindsight than on decision day: their middle cost per customer rose 297% once late credit was removed. Those 25 were picked after the study, so they show the problem’s size, not a typical campaign.

The problem

Credit counted today can belong to a budget decision made weeks ago.

Nothing is broken. Ad platforms usually file a result under the day someone clicked, so an old report keeps improving as late results trickle in. That helps you understand history and misleads you about a decision you already made.

This study measures that gap on a public ad dataset anyone can download. My part was choosing the cutoff and writing the rule down before seeing the answer.

Will this page’s numbers change? No. The study uses a closed public archive: a month of ad data that Criteo released for research, plus the month of results that followed, saved on August 20, 2026. Nothing new can arrive in it, so re-running the study gives the same numbers. A live account’s old reports keep changing.
How the correction works

Score every campaign twice: once the usual way, once with only what was known on decision day.

The finding

The gap is real, it is large, and it is not luck.

To rule out luck, I shuffled the dates and re-ran the whole test 20 times, then 999 more. No shuffled run found more than 4 campaigns worth testing; the real dates found 33. See the shuffle chart →

What changes

A correction only matters if it changes the next budget decision.

So I tested both rules on campaigns held back from the first analysis. The usual rule picks 274 campaigns. The timing-corrected rule picks a different, smaller set of 99 with about half the cost per customer, a 50.8% improvement. That is a test on public data, not proof of results in a live account.

One campaign looked 6.1× cheaper than it really was because 431 late credits were filed back into it.

A question for your own reports

Does this happen in your account?

Probably, and it is not anyone’s fault. Many ad platforms backfill conversions into the day of the click, not the day the conversion occurred.

01 Pull the same report twice. Once today, once from a snapshot 30 days ago, like the readings above. If the older numbers have changed, you have the problem.
02 Check your click-to-conversion lag. Anything longer than your reporting cycle is the size of your blind spot.
03 Replay your last budget decision. Ask what it would have looked like using only the data that existed the morning you made it.

What it took

The result only became useful after two uncomfortable checks.

How long
Built in about 3 days, going by the project’s file dates: 21 scripts in seven ordered stages, with automated tests, on a public dataset anyone can repeat.
What broke
Two bugs were caught before any result was trusted. Both were fixed, and everything was re-run without changing the plan.
