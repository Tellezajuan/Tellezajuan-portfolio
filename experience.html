In plain terms

Demand generation, in plain terms.

It’s the part of marketing that decides where advertising money goes and whether it’s actually working. I plan the campaigns, watch the numbers as they come in, and give Sales the context behind each lead. The hard part is trusting the report that says the ad worked.

Selected work

Three things from the day job I’m proud of.

Company details stay private, so some results are shown as a change rather than a raw figure.

Houghton Mifflin Harcourt · 2022 – now
Getting Sales leads worth a call The starting point. The goal was to point Sales at the leads most likely to buy. At the start, about 1 in 7 marketing-qualified leads became a sales opportunity. What I built. Reporting in GA4, Salesforce, and Marketo that tied campaigns to what happened after the handoff, plus tighter audience segments worked out with Sales. Then a lead score that weights buying-committee roles and repeat visits, and one definition of a qualified lead that Sales signed off on. Result. MQL-to-SQL conversion rose from 14% to 22% in two quarters. Sales received 18% fewer leads and opened 29% more opportunities from them.
PalmPons · 2021 – 2022
Running $65k+ a month across client accounts with a team of four The starting point. Each of our 12 client accounts had its own naming, bidding, and reporting setup, so results were hard to compare and quality depended on who owned the account. What I built. Shared campaign standards and a testing process for the team, and performance audits that turned into recommendations clients could act on. Result. 11 of 12 clients stayed through the year, and the portfolio’s average cost per lead fell 23%. How I led. Each of the four marketers owned their own accounts, we reviewed every test together in a 30-minute weekly session, and one of them moved up to account lead by the end of the year.
EZLeads · 2020 – 2021
Paid social for car dealerships The starting point. Most dealership campaigns ran one ad to a broad audience, and few leads turned into showroom visits. What I built. Targeting rebuilt around audience segments, and new creative tested on a regular schedule instead of a single version. Result. A seven-figure pipeline in my first year. Cost per lead fell from $38 to $21, and 18% of leads booked an appointment.
