How I work

I use AI to move faster without outsourcing the important decisions.

I write down the decision first, let AI speed up the repetitive building, and keep the judgment calls for myself.

Then I map the steps, look for what could break, test the result, and change course when reality disagrees. I decide what evidence is enough and when something is ready.

Where I start

Pin down the real question before picking a tool.

A request like “find the best campaigns” or “automate the search” sounds clear until someone has to decide what “best” or “automate” means. I start there.

01 / Decision What decision is this for? A report only helps if a team can do something differently because of it.
02 / What could go wrong What could make the answer misleading? Late information, missing records, testing too many things at once, and a convenient definition.
03 / My call What do I decide myself? Software can collect, compare, and flag. I decide what it can touch, what evidence is enough, and when a result is ready.
One real example

What owning the final explanation looked like when my prediction was wrong.

In the marketing study, I wrote down a prediction before the analysis ran: one week of data wouldn't be reliable enough to agree with the final answer. That was the rule before the result existed.

The result said the opposite, most of the time. I kept my original prediction, labeled it partly wrong, and left it in the record instead of quietly rewriting it. See the result →

Why this matters more than a principle Anyone can say they welcome evidence. The habit is real when the evidence contradicts your own guess and the guess stays visible.
Four habits

Think strategically. Build systemically. Test the story. Adapt on evidence.

These aren't values added after the fact. They show up in every project, from the marketing study to the game.

StrategyStart with the decision.Name the audience, the limits, and the next step before choosing a tool.
SystemsMap the whole loop.What goes in, the rules, the decisions, and what can break.
RigorTry to break the result.Test the hardest examples, keep some data aside, and write down failures.
AdaptabilityChange the plan when reality does.Keep one source of truth and show uncertainty instead of protecting an old plan.
Where AI fits

AI helps me build faster. It doesn't decide what is true.

I'm open about using AI heavily to build things. That makes checking the work more important, not less. A fast wrong answer is still wrong, and a polished screen can make a weak assumption harder to notice.

I own Judgment The question · the limits · the checks · the final explanation
AI assists Repetition Organize · repeat · compare · draft
The final call AI can speed up the work. I still decide what the result means and what happens next.
Lines I keep

Automation should make attention cheaper, not make judgment disappear.

Every project has a line it won't cross without a person.

Four areas where automation stops and a person decides.
AreaThe system doesStays with a person
MoneyTests ideas with simulated money in the research lab.A standing rule keeps it away from real money.
What counts as a matchScores job posts against a checklist.I write and revise the checklist by hand.
PublishingNarrows a huge clip queue and assembles a draft.I review and approve anything that gets posted.
BudgetExposes a timing problem in ad reports.The team still decides where the budget goes.
