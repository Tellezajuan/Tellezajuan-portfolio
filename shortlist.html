Job search workflow · written profile · shortlist

I turned hundreds of job postings into a shortlist worth reading.

A checked workflow turns roughly 120 new roles a week into a shortlist worth reading.

I built a workflow that collects roles from trusted sources and scores each one against a written profile. It narrows hundreds of postings down to the handful worth reading first. It stops at a shortlist; I read, choose, and decide what to do next.

Why I built it

Scoring is cheap. Reading is not.

A job search is mostly repetitive triage: open a listing, skim it, decide it is not close enough, move on. That work is exactly what a written profile and a scoring pass can do instead: so the roles I spend time reading are the ones worth reading.

The tool sources broadly and scores consistently, then stops at the top of the list. I read those, and I decide what to do next.

What broke first

The first run found six ways to lose a good result.

The first pipeline run pulled thousands of postings from 117 boards, but only four survived as usable jobs. The problem was not a lack of sources. Six separate filters were quietly discarding good records: source-field mapping, location classification, age filtering, title filtering, board coverage, and formula-based deduplication.

Each failure was diagnosed and fixed before the shortlist became useful. That is the professional lesson: reliable prioritization is as much about checking what disappeared as ranking what remains.

The workflow

Collect → Score → Read. The system stops before the application decision.

What "written profile" actually means

Not a black box. A list and a count.

The profile is a short list of written fit criteria: the kind of work, level of ownership, channel experience, and practical requirements that matter to me. The score is how many of those criteria show up in a posting's title or description, capped at 10. A posting that clearly matches several written criteria scores higher than one that mentions a single keyword in passing.

Why that is enough It is not trying to be clever. It is trying to be a fast, honest first pass, which is exactly the part of the job that does not need me doing it by hand across 683 postings.
What it took

A useful shortlist needed more than a score.

Repair work
The first run lost good records in six different places, so I traced each filter and repaired the handoff before trusting the ranking.
What broke
Source fields, location rules, age filters, title filters, board coverage, and deduplication each removed useful jobs.
Next time
I would measure and log every discard earlier, so a small shortlist never looks like a good result until the missing records are explained.
Why it belongs
The result is a readable 57-role snapshot from 615 scored roles, backed by a workflow that shows what disappeared and why.
