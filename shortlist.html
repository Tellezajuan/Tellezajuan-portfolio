Job search tool · personal project

I built a tool that reads hundreds of job posts so I only read the closest matches.

Job boards post about 120 new roles a week that could fit my search. The tool gathers them from 33 sources, scores each one against a short written checklist, and puts the best fits at the top.

By my rough estimate, that turns about 35 minutes a day of skimming into about 10 minutes of reading the roles worth a closer look.

See how it works See the evidence
The result 683 job posts in under six weeks; 57 made the top of the list. That is 9.3% of the 615 posts the tool could score, tracked from July 14 to August 23, 2026.

Why I built it

Scoring is cheap. Reading is not.

Most of a job search is skimming: open a posting, realize it isn't close, move on, repeat. A written checklist can do that first pass, so the time I spend reading goes to the roles that actually fit.

What broke first

The first run found six ways to lose a good posting.

The very first run searched 117 job boards and kept only four jobs. There were plenty of postings; six separate filters were quietly throwing good ones away.

I traced and fixed each one before trusting the list. The lesson: a short list only means something if you check what disappeared.

What the checklist is

Not a black box. A list and a count.

The checklist is a short list of what matters to me: the kind of work, the level of ownership, the marketing channels involved, and practical requirements. A post's score is simply how many of those show up in its title or description. A post that clearly matches several items beats one that mentions a single keyword in passing.

Why that is enough It isn't trying to be clever. It's a fast, honest first pass through 683 posts, the part of the search that doesn't need me.
What it took

A useful list needed more than a score.

How long
Built between early July and mid-August 2026, a rough estimate from the project's file dates.
Time it saves
About 10 minutes a day of reading instead of about 35 minutes of skimming. That is my rough estimate: roughly 17 new posts a day at a couple of minutes each.
Do differently
Log every post a filter throws away from day one, so a short list never looks good before the missing ones are explained.
